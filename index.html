About the Lab

Research for more accessible and data-informed urban futures

The Urban Geo-Innovation Lab explores how geospatial data science and artificial intelligence can improve the way cities are planned, measured, and understood. Led by Dr. Achituv Cohen in the Department of Civil Engineering at Ariel University, the lab works at the intersection of GIScience, urban mobility, pedestrian environments, and spatial equity — with current projects spanning inclusive routing, wheelchair and pedestrian accessibility, transport exclusion, urban digital twins, and heat exposure, in study sites across Israel, Ethiopia, Ghana, and India.

Research Themes

Core directions of the lab

GeoAI & Spatial Machine Learning

Developing intelligent geospatial methods to analyze complex urban systems using machine learning, deep learning, and network-based models.

Urban Mobility & Active Transportation

Studying walking, cycling, and micro-mobility patterns to support healthier, safer, and more sustainable cities.

Accessibility & Spatial Equity

Evaluating how urban environments serve vulnerable populations, including people with disabilities, older adults, and underserved communities.

Urban Sensing & Geospatial Data Fusion

Combining OpenStreetMap, remote sensing, LiDAR, field surveys, and mobility data to build richer and more actionable urban intelligence.

Projects

Current PhD research

Atakilti Brhanu

Transferable, explainable GeoAI for cross-city pedestrian mobility

A cross-city framework for predicting and forecasting pedestrian activity from a harmonised geodatabase of roughly 4.6 million hourly pedestrian records, built to generalise across heterogeneous urban environments and explain its own predictions.

PhD in progress
Teklebrhan Mezgebo

Wheelchair accessibility in developing cities

Standards-driven geospatial modelling of wheelchair mobility in Addis Ababa, combining field data, OpenStreetMap, DEM, and satellite imagery with a GIS-based AHP–MCDA model and CNN-extracted sidewalk widths to build a wheelchair accessibility index.

PhD in progress
Desmond Kemeh

Transport exclusion and road underdevelopment

A multi-sensor machine learning and mixed-methods study of how persistent road underdevelopment drives transport-related social exclusion in Greater Accra, Ghana, with comparative analysis across Maputo, Addis Ababa, and Tel Aviv.

PhD in progress
Stasis Mukwenya

Urban Digital Twins as a shared language for city AI

A formal ontology-based architecture for cognitive Urban Digital Twins, using learned embeddings and cross-domain attention to let energy, climate, mobility, and urban-form models enrich one another, validated on Melbourne with a Tel Aviv pilot underway.

PhD in progress
Stasis Mukwenya, with Trisalyn Nelson (UC Santa Barbara)

Topology-preserving simplification of OpenStreetMap street networks

SOD, a deterministic pipeline that turns OpenStreetMap's carriageway-level geometry into a clean, single-edge-per-street network while preserving names, classifications, and connectivity — evaluated on Turin, Tel Aviv, and San Francisco.

Under review, IJGIS
Pritipadmaja, with IIT Roorkee

Mapping urban heat stress and walkable access to green space

A composite Heat Affected Index built from two decades of MODIS land-surface-temperature data to map persistent and emerging heat-vulnerable regions across India, alongside work on walkable accessibility and exposure to urban green space.

Manuscript in preparation
